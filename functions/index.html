About This Project

Welcome to Qur'an Passage of the Day

The Qur’an itself tells us that God teaches (55:2), explains (75:19), and presents the best interpretation (25:33) of the Qur’an. Its own prescribed methodology for achieving this is known as tasreef (6:65, 6:105) - the deliberate variation of examples, narratives, and functional data points (Ayat) to ensure that a core truth is presented from multiple vantage points. For a human, the task of tracking the variations and relationships across the 6236 verses is challenging and time consuming.

As an example of the process, instead of isolating waidribuhunna in verse 4:34, the system cross-references every instance of the root d-r-b across the corpus in their contexts. The usage pattern reveals d-r-b consistently means "to set forth," "to turn away," or "to cite examples" in the internal context of the book, independent of centuries of readings layered with interpretation and culture, which too often renders waidribuhunna as "to beat".

This app harnesses the pattern-matching capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs), employing AI to build the web of meanings and words. With technology doing the heavy lifting, your mind is freed and empowered to learn, reflect deeply – engage in tadabbur – and continue your journey of personal, spiritual and moral edification. For just as tasreef governs scripture, so it appears in the dynamic, recurring patterns of Nature. And our aspiration is to foster a deeper relationship between you and the Qur’anic text, thus with the observable cosmos.

Each day, this app presents a randomly selected passage in its immediate context, preserving the natural thematic flow of the text. The passage is displayed with a standard translation - in English by Dr Safi Kaskas, or Bahasa Melayu by Othman Ali - or you can choose the academic tasreef-derived rendering in either language. The app then provides a Thematic Overview based on tasreef by the AI semantic engine, followed by morphological analysis of key words of the passage.

Note: In Arabic grammar, a triliteral root (al-jathr) carries the fundamental, broad meaning of a word family, while the pattern weight (al-wazn) acts as a standardized template determining exact grammatical function. The Key Morphological Analysis applies this dual framework systematically across the text. By isolating recurring roots and their derived forms, it uncovers semantic clusters across disparate verses or sentences, serving as the foundational mechanism by which the app generates its thematic overview.

Powered by Gemini 3.1 and the QuranExplorer linguistic framework. Audio from the Quran API project (https://quranapi.pages.dev/)
